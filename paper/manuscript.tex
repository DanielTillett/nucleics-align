\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} Exact pairwise sequence alignment with affine gap penalties (Needleman--Wunsch, Smith--Waterman, Gotoh) remains necessary whenever heuristic mappers may miss optimal alignments or when reproducible scores and fixed tie-breaking are required. Classic scalar tools such as Isaac Turner's \texttt{seq-align} are simple and exact, but aligning a 20~kb DNA pair takes $\sim$11~s and 4.5~GiB of RAM, and a 50~kb pair would require $\sim$28~GiB. Modern libraries (\texttt{parasail}, \texttt{ksw2}, \texttt{SSW}, \texttt{WFA2}, \texttt{edlib}, \texttt{SeqAn}, \texttt{EMBOSS}) each address parts of this space, but standalone C tools combining general substitution matrices, single-pair multi-threading, linear-space global alignment at high divergence, and $O(s)$-space bidirectional wavefront alignment remain scarce.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Results:} I present Nucleics Align (v1.2.0), a standalone C11 library and CLI suite derived from \texttt{seq-align}. It combines 1-byte-per-cell traceback storage, portable striped SIMD kernels (SSE2, SSE4.1, AVX2, AVX-512, Arm64 NEON) with prefix-scan carries and cache-line-padded multi-threading, 8- and 16-bit difference-encoded lanes, formally certified diagonal banding with strip pruning and strip-kept memory, linear-space Hirschberg/Myers--Miller alignment with row reuse (\texttt{-{}-linear}), $O(s)$-space bidirectional wavefront alignment (\texttt{-{}-wfa}), and Myers' bit-vector edit distance for unit-cost \texttt{-{}-scoreonly}. Every default optimisation reproduces the exact optimal score and tie-breaking of this codebase's scalar Gotoh baseline (optional \texttt{-{}-linear} and \texttt{-{}-wfa} guarantee the identical optimal score), verified across $\sim$2.0~million unit-test checks (including ASan/UBSan) and 63~CLI fixtures (75~runs). On native Linux (8-core AMD Ryzen~7 5700G), Nucleics Align is 45--424$\times$ faster than original \texttt{seq-align} on 20~kb global DNA on 1~thread (25.9--248.2~ms vs 11.0--12.1~s) and 153--414$\times$ faster on 8~threads (26.5--73.7~ms). On 1~thread, \texttt{-{}-wfa} aligns 20~kb and 50~kb 1\% pairs in 11.3~ms (2.8~MiB) and 47.6~ms (3.3~MiB), $1.6\times$ faster and 10--37$\times$ smaller than \texttt{WFA2} high-memory mode; \texttt{-{}-linear} is 2.3--14.8$\times$ faster than \texttt{ksw2} across all six 20--50~kb conditions using 2.8--6.5~MiB (138--690$\times$ less RAM). A fresh-process nine-configuration ablation quantifies each layer's runtime and peak RAM. On real RefSeq/GenBank pairs (8~threads), \texttt{-{}-wfa} aligns 197~kb Mpox genomes (99.92\% identity) in 8.1~ms / 4.3~MiB, \texttt{-{}-linear} aligns SARS-CoV-2 vs SARS-CoV-1 (29.9~kb, 79.54\% identity) in 127.0~ms / 4.5~MiB and 580~kb \textit{Mycoplasma genitalium} chromosomes in 2.28~s / 29.2~MiB, and five BLOSUM62 protein orthologues (390--3,066~aa) run up to $28.4\times$ faster than \texttt{seq-align} in 2.0--8.1~ms.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Nucleics Align makes exact affine-gap global alignment practical up to megabase scale and accelerates local and protein alignment on ordinary desktop hardware, without external alignment dependencies.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Availability:} Source code, C library, CLI tools, Python~3 and Perl interfaces, and reproducible benchmark harnesses are released under the BSD 3-Clause licence at \url{https://github.com/DanielTillett/nucleics-align}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Exact pairwise sequence alignment by dynamic programming has been a core method in bioinformatics since the 1970s. Needleman and Wunsch introduced optimal global alignment \citep{needleman1970general}, Smith and Waterman formulated optimal local alignment \citep{smith1981identification}, and Gotoh reduced affine gap-penalty alignment from cubic to quadratic time using three coupled dynamic programming matrices \citep{gotoh1982improved}. Under an affine gap model, a contiguous insertion or deletion of $n$ residues scores $g_{\text{open}} + n \cdot g_{\text{extend}}$, penalising gap initiation more heavily than extension so that one contiguous indel is preferred over scattered single-base gaps.

In high-throughput genomics, heuristic seed-and-extend tools such as BLAST/BLAST+ \citep{altschul1990blast,camacho2009blastplus} and minimap2 \citep{li2018minimap2} dominate database search and read mapping because $O(mn)$ dynamic programming is prohibitive across millions of reads or gigabase references. Exact pairwise alignment remains necessary, however, when comparing viral and organelle genomes, validating variants near complex indels, scoring homologous proteins with BLOSUM \citep{henikoff1992amino} or PAM \citep{dayhoff1978model} matrices, or establishing ground-truth reference alignments when testing heuristic tools.

Over the past five decades, four main families of exact speed-ups have been developed:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SIMD vectorisation and multi-threading.} Early implementations exploited multimedia instructions along anti-diagonals \citep{wozniak1997using} or query profiles \citep{rognes2000sixfold}. Farrar's striped SIMD layout \citep{farrar2007striped} moved the vertical-gap dependency out of the inner loop into a lazy-F correction pass and underpins \texttt{SSW} \citep{zhao2013ssw,sswrepo} and \texttt{parasail} \citep{daily2016parasail}. Prefix-scan carry propagation was introduced for GPU Smith--Waterman by Khajeh-Saeed et~al.~\citep{khajehsaeed2010acceleration}, implemented in \texttt{parasail}'s scan kernels \citep{daily2016parasail}, and used inside adaptive SIMD blocks by Block Aligner \citep{liu2023block}. Suzuki and Kasahara \citep{suzuki2018introducing} showed that storing vertical and horizontal score differences keeps lane values within 8 or 16~bits regardless of sequence length, an approach used in \texttt{ksw2} \citep{li2018minimap2,ksw2repo}. The C++ \texttt{SeqAn} library \citep{rahn2018seqan} combines vectorised DP with multi-threading across and within sequence pairs.
  \item \textbf{Linear-space divide-and-conquer.} Hirschberg \citep{hirschberg1975linear} and Myers and Miller \citep{myers1988optimal} reduced the working memory of exact global alignment from $O(mn)$ to linear in sequence length at the cost of roughly twice the arithmetic work, later combined with diagonal banding by Chao et~al.~\citep{chao1992aligning}. Many biologists encounter this algorithm via EMBOSS \texttt{stretcher} (alongside full-matrix \texttt{needle}) \citep{rice2000emboss}.
  \item \textbf{Output-sensitive and volume-pruning methods.} Ukkonen \citep{ukkonen1985algorithms} showed that edit distance can be computed in $O(s \cdot \min(m, n))$ time for edit distance $s$, and Spouge \citep{spouge1989speeding} formalised computational volumes for pruning unreachable lattice states. For affine gaps, the Wavefront Alignment (WFA) algorithm \citep{marcosola2021fast,wfa2lib} runs in $O(ns)$ worst-case time and $O(s^2)$ space; Eizenga and Paten (preprint) \citep{eizenga2022improving} generalised its scoring and analysed an $O(n + s^2)$ expected-time variant under uniform random errors; bidirectional WFA (\texttt{BiWFA}) reduces space to $O(s)$ \citep{marcosola2023optimal}; and \texttt{Singletrack} \citep{lopezvillellas2026singletrack} cuts affine backtrace memory $3\times$ inside \texttt{WFA2-lib}. Groot Koerkamp's \texttt{A*PA2} \citep{grootkoerkamp2024apa2} applies admissible heuristics and strip-based computational volumes to unit-cost edit distance.
  \item \textbf{Bit-vector parallelism.} For unit-cost Levenshtein edit distance, Myers' bit-vector algorithm \citep{myers1999fast} computes 64~DP cells per machine word using a small constant number of bitwise operations and underlies \texttt{edlib} \citep{sosic2017edlib}.
\end{enumerate}

Each existing tool occupies a distinct niche. \texttt{edlib} \citep{sosic2017edlib} is limited to unit-cost edit distance without affine gaps or substitution matrices. \texttt{WFA2} and \texttt{BiWFA} \citep{marcosola2021fast,marcosola2023optimal} are fast on closely related pairs, but their work grows with penalty score $s$ so they slow sharply on divergent or unrelated 20--50~kb pairs, and they do not support arbitrary protein substitution matrices. \texttt{parasail} \citep{daily2016parasail} and \texttt{ksw2} \citep{li2018minimap2} support general scoring well, but run single-threaded per pair and consume gigabytes of RAM on 50~kb global alignments. \texttt{SSW} \citep{zhao2013ssw} is fast for local alignment but does not provide global affine-gap alignment. \texttt{EMBOSS} \texttt{needle} and \texttt{stretcher} \citep{rice2000emboss} are scalar implementations, while \texttt{SeqAn} \citep{rahn2018seqan} is a C++ template library rather than a lightweight standalone C tool.

I started Nucleics Align by forking Isaac Turner's \texttt{seq-align} \citep{turner2017seqalign} for its exact Gotoh formulation, fixed tie-breaking, and minimal C dependency footprint. Its limitation was resource use: on a modern desktop, aligning a single 20~kb DNA pair took $\sim$11~s and 4.5~GiB of RAM, and a 50~kb pair would have required $\sim$28~GiB. Nucleics Align keeps \texttt{seq-align}'s exact mathematics and scoring flexibility while integrating all four families of exact speed-ups into one portable C11 library and CLI suite. The engineering rule throughout has been strict: every default optimisation must produce the exact same score and alignment, byte for byte, as this codebase's scalar Gotoh baseline (the few intentional bug fixes relative to the original 2017 \texttt{seq-align} are listed in \texttt{CHANGELOG.md}).

\section{Implementation}

Nucleics Align is written in portable C11 with no external alignment library dependencies (bundling only Isaac Turner's \texttt{string\_buffer}, \texttt{seq\_file}, and \texttt{sort\_r}, plus \texttt{zlib} and POSIX threads). It builds on Linux, macOS, and Windows under WSL (native Windows compilers are not supported), and provides three CLI tools (\texttt{needleman\_wunsch}, \texttt{smith\_waterman}, \texttt{lcs}), a static C library (\texttt{libalign.a}), a zero-dependency Python~3 wrapper, and Perl modules.

\subsection{Execution dispatch and modes}

Global alignment in \texttt{needleman\_wunsch} dispatches according to the requested mode and scoring options:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Score-only (\texttt{-{}-scoreonly}):} Invokes the checked linear-space score pass. Where scoring options are supported, it first tries a banded striped SIMD pass; if banding does not pay, it attempts Myers' bit-vector algorithm \citep{myers1999fast} under unit-cost scoring (\texttt{0/-1/0/-1}) on one thread (or when SIMD is unavailable), falling back to a full striped or scalar linear pass. On unsupported scoring (such as free terminal gaps or positive gap-open penalties), it falls back to the full global aligner; other errors terminate with an error status.
  \item \textbf{Explicit linear space (\texttt{-{}-linear}):} Calls the checked Hirschberg/Myers--Miller aligner directly and exits non-zero on unsupported options or errors.
  \item \textbf{Default global alignment:} When \texttt{-{}-wfa} is enabled, it first attempts budgeted bidirectional wavefront alignment; if \texttt{-{}-linearabove <n>} is set and the longer sequence has at least $n$ letters, it attempts linear-space alignment. If either is unsupported or does not complete, execution falls back to the matrix aligner. The matrix aligner uses a compact 1-byte-per-cell traceback fill whenever \texttt{opts.striped} is enabled and the scoring options are supported by the striped kernels: it first attempts a short budgeted score-only WFA pass (default budget 16~diagonal updates per letter of \texttt{seq\_a}) or 12-mer estimate to size an exact band (Section~\ref{sec:banding}), falling back to a full difference-encoded matrix fill if banding does not pay, and to the scalar three-matrix fill if \texttt{opts.striped} is disabled or scoring options are unsupported by the striped kernels. If \texttt{-{}-linearfallback} is enabled and matrix allocation fails or overflows \texttt{size\_t}, it falls back to linear space.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compact traceback storage and deterministic tie-breaking}

The baseline Gotoh implementation stores three 32-bit score matrices (\texttt{MATCH}, \texttt{GAP\_A}, \texttt{GAP\_B}), requiring 12~bytes per cell ($\sim$4.47~GiB for $20{,}000 \times 20{,}000$ cells). In Nucleics Align, compact mode (default in the CLI whenever \texttt{opts.striped} is enabled) packs the three 2-bit predecessor state indicators into one byte per cell while keeping only two score rows in RAM during the forward fill, cutting matrix memory 12-fold. Above 1~MiB, traceback buffers grow by $1.25\times$ rather than power-of-two doubling to avoid over-allocating multi-gigabyte matrices.

Whenever multiple paths tie for the optimal score, traceback in every full-matrix path (scalar matrices, compact traceback bytes, exact bands, strip-kept bands, and difference-encoded lanes) follows the same deterministic rule. In Gotoh traceback, the current matrix state fixes the spatial step (\texttt{GAP\_A} steps to $(x, y-1)$, \texttt{GAP\_B} to $(x-1, y)$, \texttt{MATCH} to $(x-1, y-1)$), and at that predecessor cell the next state is chosen in strict priority order \texttt{GAP\_A}, \texttt{GAP\_B}, \texttt{MATCH}. Global alignment starts at $(|A|, |B|)$ using the same state priority; Smith--Waterman always starts in \texttt{MATCH} at the highest-scoring cell (breaking ties by lowest end coordinate $x$, then $y$). This priority tends to push gaps toward the right end of runs (e.g., \texttt{AAA} vs \texttt{AA} gives \texttt{AA-}, and \texttt{ACCA} vs \texttt{ACA} gives \texttt{AC-A}). By contrast, \texttt{-{}-linear} and \texttt{-{}-wfa} always return the identical optimal score but may pick a different equal-scoring alignment due to midpoint or wavefront tie-breaking.

\subsection{Portable striped SIMD, prefix-scan carries, and narrow/difference-encoded lanes}

All vectorised kernels use portable GCC/clang vector extensions rather than architecture-specific intrinsics. On x86-64 Linux, GCC/clang function multi-versioning (\texttt{target\_clones} for \texttt{avx2}, \texttt{sse4.1}, and \texttt{default} SSE2) compiles all three variants into one portable binary (8-lane 32-bit vectors under GCC; 4-lane under Clang dispatch, as Clang rejects 8-lane vectors inside \texttt{target\_clones} multi-versioned functions). Building with \texttt{NATIVE=1} (\texttt{-march=native}) enables $16 \times 32$-bit AVX-512 lanes where present, and Arm64 builds (including Apple silicon) use NEON automatically. On non-Linux x86-64 builds without \texttt{NATIVE=1}, multi-versioning is disabled and the build uses baseline SSE2 (with linear-space passes falling back to scalar code).

The striped fill follows Farrar's layout \citep{farrar2007striped}, precomputing a per-query substitution profile for the active alphabet. Drawing on prefix-scan carry propagation \citep{khajehsaeed2010acceleration,daily2016parasail,liu2023block}, Nucleics Align precomputes inter-lane vertical-gap carries in a single scan rather than iterating multi-sweep lazy-F loops across banded rows. To increase lane density without losing exactness: global full traceback fills store vertical and horizontal score differences \citep{suzuki2018introducing}, keeping values in 8-bit lanes when the per-step score range fits (including standard DNA scoring) and 16-bit lanes for wider matrices such as BLOSUM62 at any sequence length; local fills begin in 8-bit lanes (four times as many lanes as 32-bit vectors) and widen to 16- or 32-bit lanes only if a score threatens to saturate; and linear-space passes use 16-bit relative score blocks.

\subsection{Exact certified banding, strip pruning, and strip-kept memory}
\label{sec:banding}

Let $m = |A|$, $n = |B|$, $D = m - n$, $s_{\max} = \max\big(0, \max_{a,b} S(a,b)\big)$, and assume standard affine gap scoring $g_{\text{open}} + \ell \cdot g_{\text{extend}}$ with fully penalised terminal gaps, non-positive penalties $g_{\text{open}} \le 0$ and $g_{\text{extend}} \le 0$, and $s_{\max} - 2 g_{\text{extend}} > 0$. Before filling a banded global matrix, Nucleics Align first attempts a budgeted score-only WFA pass \citep{marcosola2021fast} (default 16~diagonal updates per letter of $A$); when it finishes, it yields the exact optimal score. Otherwise, a fast 12-mer sampling routine produces a deliberately high heuristic score estimate used solely to decide whether a band is worth trying; the final alignment never depends on that estimate.

Exactness is certified mathematically from the score achieved inside the closed band $B(b) = \{(x, y) : \min(0, D) - b \le x - y \le \max(0, D) + b\}$. Let $S_{\text{out}}(b)$ denote the maximum score of any global path that leaves $B(b)$ (or $-\infty$ if no such path exists). Any global path visiting a cell outside $B(b)$ requires at least $I = |D| + 2(b + 1)$ gap characters and at most $(m + n - I)/2 = \min(m, n) - (b + 1)$ aligned residue pairs; because $g_{\text{open}} \le 0$ and $s_{\max} \ge 0$, every path leaving $B(b)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  S_{\text{out}}(b) \le s_{\max}\big(\min(m, n) - (b + 1)\big) + g_{\text{extend}}\big(|D| + 2(b + 1)\big).
\end{equation}
Consequently, once a banded fill finds a feasible global alignment of score $L$, every path leaving $B(b)$ satisfies the strict inequality $S_{\text{out}}(b) < L$ (excluding both better and tied optimal paths outside $B(b)$) whenever:
\begin{equation}
  b \ge \left\lfloor \frac{s_{\max}\min(m, n) + g_{\text{extend}}|D| - L}{s_{\max} - 2 g_{\text{extend}}} \right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Excluded predecessor cells outside $B(b)$ are set to the DP floor so no cell score inside $B(b)$ is ever overestimated. When this inequality holds, every optimal and tied-optimal path stays within the closed band $B(b)$, preserving byte-identical full-matrix traceback. If the certificate fails, the band is widened and re-run until the inequality holds, or abandoned for a full matrix fill (which is exact unconditionally).

Inspired by \texttt{A*PA2} \citep{grootkoerkamp2024apa2} and Spouge's computational volumes \citep{spouge1989speeding}, the band is filled in horizontal strips. Once a feasible lower bound $L$ is known (from the exact WFA score or from a prior banded pass) and $g_{\text{extend}} < 0$, for each cell $(x, y)$ on the bottom row of a strip with best state score $v = \max(M[x,y], G_A[x,y], G_B[x,y])$ and remaining lengths $r_i = m - x$, $r_j = n - y$, any completion to $(m, n)$ scores at most:
\begin{equation}
  U(x, y) = v + s_{\max}\min(r_i, r_j) + g_{\text{extend}}|r_i - r_j|.
\end{equation}
Because $s_{\max} \ge 0$, $U(x, y)$ remains a valid upper bound even when all substitution scores are negative. Cells with $U(x, y) < L$ cannot lie on any optimal or tied-optimal path and are pruned; each surviving cell on diagonal $d = x - y$ can reach at most $k = \lfloor (U(x, y) - L) / (-2 g_{\text{extend}}) \rfloor$ diagonals beyond $[\min(d, D), \max(d, D)]$. When the estimated band footprint exceeds one quarter of physical RAM (falling back to 1~GiB when physical memory cannot be queried), the aligner switches automatically to strip-kept mode, retaining only strip boundary rows during the forward pass and refilling one strip's traceback bytes at a time during traceback. For a 1~Mb, 1\% nominal mutation global alignment on a 62~GiB host, this avoids an estimated $\sim$35~GiB band allocation and runs in 319~MiB while preserving full-matrix tie-breaking.

\subsection{Budgeted bidirectional wavefront alignment (\texttt{-{}-wfa})}

When \texttt{-{}-wfa} is requested, Nucleics Align converts affine match/mismatch/gap scoring into non-negative penalty costs \citep{eizenga2022improving} and advances wavefronts by penalty score \citep{marcosola2021fast}. For short pairs ($|A| + |B| \le 2{,}048$), it traces back directly through retained wavefronts. For larger pairs, v1.2.0 implements $O(s)$-space bidirectional wavefront alignment (\texttt{BiWFA}) \citep{marcosola2023optimal}: forward and reverse ring-buffer wavefronts advance alternately until they meet across match or affine gap states (accounting for the shared gap-open charge on $I$--$I$ and $D$--$D$ breakpoints), recursively splitting the problem down to the retained-wavefront base case. Each wavefront step decouples its branch-free interior compute loop (compiled with \texttt{target\_clones} for AVX2, SSE4.1, and SSE2) from its 8-byte word-comparison match-extension loop, and skips overlap checks until the sum of forward and reverse furthest offsets reaches $|B|$. To fall back quickly on divergent pairs, the global aligner caps total diagonal updates at $\min(2^{28},\; 256 \cdot (|A| + |B|))$; if scoring does not reduce to one match score, one mismatch score, and standard affine gap penalties, or if the work budget is exhausted, execution falls back to the matrix aligner.

\subsection{Linear-space global alignment (\texttt{-{}-linear}) and Myers bit-vector score-only}

The \texttt{-{}-linear} aligner implements Hirschberg's divide-and-conquer algorithm \citep{hirschberg1975linear} extended to Gotoh's affine gap model \citep{myers1988optimal}, combined with certified banded striped passes \citep{chao1992aligning,farrar2007striped}, 16-bit relative blocks, and two-level intermediate row reuse so child sub-problems skip redundant passes. Working score-row memory is $O(|B|)$ (with constant-factor overhead for row reuse and per-thread buffers), plus $O(|A| + |B|)$ for the returned alignment strings.

For \texttt{-{}-scoreonly}, when scoring is unit-cost edit distance (\texttt{0/-1/0/-1}), banding does not pay, and execution is single-threaded (or SIMD is unavailable), Nucleics Align dispatches to Myers' bit-vector algorithm \citep{myers1999fast}, computing 64~cells per machine word using a small constant number of bitwise operations.

\subsection{Best-hit local alignment (\texttt{smith\_waterman -{}-maxhits 1})}

When only the single highest-scoring local hit is requested (\texttt{-{}-maxhits 1}), Nucleics Align scans forward in $O(|B|)$ memory to locate the optimal end cell and score, then scans in reverse from that cell in $O(|B|)$ memory to locate the start cell. Only the resulting sub-rectangle is filled for traceback, with periodic row checkpoints if the rectangle exceeds $2^{28}$ cells. During checkpointed strip refills, v1.2.0 reuses the precomputed substitution table and selects 8- or 16-bit SIMD lanes whenever the known optimal score fits safely, preserving byte-identical traceback.

\subsection{Multi-threading, error handling, and verification}

By default, the CLI tools use half of the host's logical CPUs (\texttt{-{}-threads <n>} overrides this). Large single alignments (exceeding $4 \times 10^6$ cells and 1,024~columns per thread block) split columns into pipelined wavefront blocks across threads, while file batches (\texttt{-{}-file}, \texttt{-{}-files}) align independent pairs concurrently and emit results in strict input order so output is byte-identical to \texttt{-{}-threads 1}. Per-block progress counters are 64-byte aligned (\texttt{\_Alignas(64)}) to prevent cache-line false sharing, waiters cache the last observed row to avoid redundant atomic loads, and progress is published every 16~rows with x86 \texttt{pause} hints during short spins.

In the C library, every major aligner provides a checked entry point returning an error status (or \texttt{1}/\texttt{0} with an \texttt{aln\_error\_t} parameter for \texttt{smith\_waterman\_fetch\_checked()}) instead of calling \texttt{exit()}. Continuous integration runs on Linux (x86-64 and Arm64) and macOS with GCC and Clang, including AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer (\texttt{SANITIZE=1 make test}), $\sim$2.0~million unit-test assertion checks (many on randomised inputs comparing every optimised path against the scalar Gotoh baseline), and 63~CLI regression fixtures (75~test runs, as 12~batch fixtures run at both 1 and 4~threads).

\section{Results}

All main benchmarks were run on a native Linux desktop (AMD Ryzen~7 5700G, 8~cores / 16~logical CPUs, 62~GiB RAM, Pop!\_OS 24.04 Linux, GCC~13.3.0, default \texttt{make} \texttt{-O3} build; CPU frequency governor \texttt{powersave}, SMT active, no manual core pinning). Raw per-seed timings, full SHA-256 input and binary digests, and exact build metadata for Tables~\ref{tab:real}, \ref{tab:ablation}, \ref{tab:compare}, and \ref{tab:protein} (Nucleics Align v1.2.0, commit \texttt{aaf0754}) are archived in \texttt{benchmarks/results/pop-os-v1.2.0.txt}. CLI timings include process startup, input parsing, alignment, and output writing (so sub-10~ms runs include $\sim$2--4~ms of process startup overhead); the C library ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) times the alignment call directly via \texttt{clock\_gettime(CLOCK\_MONOTONIC)}. Memory figures report peak resident set size (RSS) from GNU \texttt{time -v} in binary MiB/GiB ($1~\text{MiB} = 1024^2$~bytes; $1~\text{GiB} = 1024$~MiB), and CPU times come from GNU \texttt{time -v} (10~ms resolution, where \texttt{0.00~s} is displayed as $<$10~ms). Unless noted, global DNA runs used \texttt{+1} match, \texttt{-2} mismatch, \texttt{-4} gap open, \texttt{-1} gap extend; local DNA runs used \texttt{+2/-2/-2/-1}; and protein runs used \texttt{-{}-scoring BLOSUM62} (\texttt{-10/-1}). All global modes and comparators in Tables~\ref{tab:real}, \ref{tab:ablation}, \ref{tab:compare}, and \ref{tab:protein} were programmatically asserted to return the identical optimal score on every completed pair.

\subsection{Real biological sequences}

Seven public NCBI RefSeq/GenBank pairs spanning mitochondrial genomes, coronaviruses, orthopoxviruses, whole bacterial chromosomes, and mammalian proteins were timed with 8~default threads on v1.2.0 (best of 3~runs for pairs $\le 200$~kb, 1~run for \textit{M.~genitalium}; reproducible via \texttt{benchmarks/real\_sequences.sh}; Table~\ref{tab:real}). Circular mitochondrial and bacterial genomes were aligned as deposited in RefSeq without origin rotation; the 580~kb \textit{M.~genitalium} pair serves as a whole-chromosome scaling stress test rather than a rearrangement-aware comparison. Reported sequence identities belong to the \texttt{full} traceback and count case-insensitive equal symbols divided by total alignment columns (so \texttt{N/N} counts as an identity match under \texttt{-{}-pretty}, whereas under default DNA alignment scoring ambiguous bases such as \texttt{N} are scored as mismatches).

\section{Discussion}

Exact pairwise alignment has often forced a choice between general-purpose DP libraries that are predictable but slow and memory-hungry on long sequences, and output-sensitive wavefront aligners that are fast on near-identical inputs but degrade sharply on divergent ones. Nucleics Align combines compact traceback storage, difference-encoded striped SIMD with prefix-scan carry propagation, formally certified strip banding, Hirschberg/Myers--Miller linear space, and $O(s)$-space bidirectional WFA inside one portable C11 codebase, giving predictable performance from kilobase proteins and viral genomes up to megabase DNA pairs.

\subsection{Novelty and engineering contributions}

Nucleics Align builds on established algorithmic foundations \citep{gotoh1982improved,hirschberg1975linear,myers1988optimal,farrar2007striped,suzuki2018introducing,marcosola2021fast,marcosola2023optimal,myers1999fast,grootkoerkamp2024apa2}. Its primary contributions are engineering and systems-level: (1)~a closed-band exactness certificate (Section~\ref{sec:banding}) that excludes tied optimal paths outside the band, combined with strip-level upper-bound pruning and automatic strip-kept memory fallback so full-matrix tie-breaking is preserved byte-for-byte even when a band would otherwise exceed RAM; and (2)~the unified integration of all four exact speed-up families---with portable multi-versioned SIMD, cache-line-padded single-pair multi-threading, and full general substitution-matrix support---into a zero-external-alignment-dependency C11 library and CLI suite.

Nucleics Align is not a replacement for seed-and-extend read mappers such as \texttt{minimap2} \citep{li2018minimap2} or database search tools such as \texttt{BLAST+} \citep{camacho2009blastplus}, nor for \texttt{edlib} \citep{sosic2017edlib} when only unit-cost edit distance is needed (as confirmed by Table~\ref{tab:compare}). It targets workflows that require exact affine-gap global, overlap, or local alignment---including custom substitution matrices, wildcards, and fixed tie-breaking---from both the command line and C, Python, or Perl. Recent advances such as \texttt{Singletrack} \citep{lopezvillellas2026singletrack} inside \texttt{WFA2-lib} reduce wavefront backtrace memory $3\times$ and remain complementary for wavefront-dominated workloads.

\subsection{Limitations and trade-offs}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Spin-waiting CPU overhead:} Multi-threaded single-pair fills synchronise column blocks by spinning (even after v1.2.0's 64-byte padding and 16-row batched publication), so total CPU time on 8~threads can still exceed single-threaded comparators on full-matrix fills (e.g., 3.14~s CPU vs 2.34~s for \texttt{ksw2} on 50~kb unrelated \texttt{full}, despite 414~ms vs 2,355~ms wall time). By contrast, \texttt{-{}-linear} on 8~threads uses \textit{less} total CPU time than \texttt{ksw2} (0.79--1.17~s vs 2.33--2.34~s), and \texttt{-{}-threads 1} avoids spin overhead altogether on busy multi-job servers.
  \item \textbf{Local alignment remains $O(mn)$ in time:} Because \texttt{smith\_waterman -{}-maxhits 1} uses neither banding nor WFA, long similar local pairs remain quadratic in time (e.g., 9.4~s and 626~MiB on the 197~kb Mpox pair vs 8.1~ms for global \texttt{-{}-wfa}).
  \item \textbf{WFA crossover and scoring scope:} \texttt{-{}-wfa} applies only when scoring reduces to one match score, one mismatch score, and standard affine gap penalties. Although v1.2.0 BiWFA removes the retained-wavefront memory ceiling and aligns 50~kb 1\% pairs in 47.6~ms / 3.3~MiB, on divergent ($\ge 10\%$) or unrelated pairs its work budget is exhausted and it falls back to the matrix aligner; it therefore remains opt-in.
  \item \textbf{Memory vs tie-breaking on large pairs:} Full compact alignment on 580~kb \textit{M.~genitalium} still uses 4.33~GiB, and strip-kept mode on 1~Mb 1\% pairs incurs refill overhead (8.10~s vs 6.40~s for \texttt{-{}-linear} in Table~\ref{tab:scaling}; 7.92~s vs 6.33~s in the separate strip run). \texttt{-{}-linear} and \texttt{-{}-wfa} avoid this memory cost while returning the identical optimal score, but may select a different equal-scoring alignment when ties exist.
  \item \textbf{Single-piece affine gaps, synthetic indel model, and platform dispatch:} Nucleics Align implements Gotoh's single-piece affine gap model rather than the dual-affine convex gap cost used by \texttt{minimap2}/\texttt{ksw2} \citep{li2018minimap2}. Synthetic benchmarks in Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}--\ref{tab:compare} model single-base indels rather than long block indels (which are represented in the real viral and protein pairs in Tables~\ref{tab:real} and \ref{tab:protein}). Automatic AVX2/SSE4.1/SSE2 multi-versioning applies on x86-64 Linux; macOS and non-Linux x86-64 builds require \texttt{NATIVE=1} for AVX2/AVX-512.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future work}

Two planned extensions build directly on the current architecture: combining Myers' bit-vector passes \citep{myers1999fast} with Hirschberg's divide-and-conquer \citep{hirschberg1975linear} so unit-cost \texttt{-{}-linear} gains 64-cell-per-word throughput for full alignments, and exposing \texttt{-{}-wfa} and \texttt{-{}-linearabove} in the Python wrapper.
